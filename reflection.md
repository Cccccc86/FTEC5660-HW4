# When everyone has an AI agent

Chen Youxin · 1155274414  
FTEC5660 · Homework 4 Reflection

Ten years from now, if everyone has a powerful AI assistant, I think I would use mine often. AI can already do some things much faster than I can. It can read several long files at once, pull scattered information together, or write a short script to clean up data. I would like to make good use of that. What worries me is getting into the habit of asking AI as soon as I run into a problem. Sometimes I have barely worked out what I am asking when it already has an explanation that sounds right. If that happens often enough, I might start taking its first answer as my own judgment. I still want to give myself a moment to think, so I know what I agree with, what I doubt and why.

I think many of the things I want to do now will still matter to me. I want to understand how businesses work and how people make financial decisions. I would also like to turn some of what I learn into tools people can use. I still have a lot to learn, and AI can help along the way. I can ask it to gather information, organise figures from financial reports, or take care of repetitive technical tasks. This homework gave me a small example. The same company's revenue may appear as a rounded number in the shareholder letter and as a precise figure in the financial statements. AI can find a number quickly. I still need to check where it came from, which year it refers to and whether it answers the question. I hope to use the time I save to understand those things better.

I already feel that what someone can get done is only one part of what makes them valuable. I expect I'll feel the same way in ten years. I also think about a person's character, how much effort they put in, what they have a talent for and the unusual ideas they sometimes come up with. It matters to me how they make plans, handle a surprise and treat other people along the way. Those are things I would look for in myself, too. Knowing how to use AI may become another useful skill, and I do want to be among the people who use it well. Even with the same tool, some people will ask better questions, some will know how to check the results, and some will turn an odd idea into something real. Outside work, I hope my friends would say I keep my word and listen when they need to talk.

I see people using AI to make short dramas now. I get the appeal. It gives them a way to put stories on screen that would have been difficult to film before. For my own projects, I would like to build things that help people solve a real problem. I could make a tool for someone who is just learning to read financial reports, for example. It could show them the original table, make the units clear and let them check the number for themselves. Even if only a few people used it, I would think it was worth the time if it helped them when they got stuck.

I also want to keep some of the harder parts of learning. When I am debugging, AI can suggest a fix almost immediately. If I copy it and stop as soon as the program runs, I might still have no idea where to look the next time something similar breaks. Starting tomorrow, before I ask AI about a problem, I want to write down my best guess and one way to test it. If I get stuck, I can ask for another explanation and try it out myself. If AI turns out to be right, I want to understand what it noticed that I missed. Over time, I hope that will make me better at using AI and better at thinking through problems myself.
